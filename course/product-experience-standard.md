# AI Kollege Paid Product Experience Standard

This standard defines the customer value that must distinguish a paid AI Kollege learning product from generic free AI information.

## Value contract

Paid value is not access to facts about AI. Paid value is a guided transformation:

**diagnose → personalize → practice → produce → verify → improve → prove → continue**

Every paid experience must help the learner leave with useful work they created, evidence of review, and a next-step plan. Marketing must never promise a job, income, accreditation, licensing, compliance status, or a business result that the product cannot prove.

## Adaptive onboarding

Before paid instruction begins, collect only the minimum useful context:

- learner goal: work better, get job-ready, change role, support a business, or learn safe foundations;
- current or target role/industry;
- self-rated AI experience;
- repeated task or problem they want to improve;
- confidence with checking AI output;
- privacy/sensitive-data context;
- accessibility or preferred learning pace where the interface supports it.

Reuse information already supplied through the readiness diagnostic. Do not ask the learner to repeat known answers.

The learner receives:

1. a concise readiness/skill-gap summary;
2. recommended starting point;
3. modules that can be reviewed quickly when evidence shows competence;
4. extra practice where diagnostic evidence shows weakness;
5. examples and project choices relevant to the learner's role.

Adaptation changes examples, practice, recommendations and project choices. It must not silently lower completion standards.

## Learning UX

Each module should follow a predictable pattern:

1. **Why this matters** — one practical outcome.
2. **See it** — a realistic example.
3. **Try it** — guided practice with a template or scaffold.
4. **Check it** — verification, safety or quality check.
5. **Use it** — apply it to the learner's own work context.
6. **Save proof** — retain the artifact/evidence for the final proof pack.

Requirements:

- avoid walls of text when a checklist, example, decision aid or exercise is clearer;
- provide an example before blank-input tasks;
- use plain-language instructions and define unavoidable jargon;
- show progress based on completed work, not page views;
- preserve work-in-progress where technically supported;
- make the next action obvious after every completed step;
- do not force an upsell to continue a free or already-purchased entitlement;
- do not recommend a paid upgrade when the learner's demonstrated need does not justify it.

## Role adaptation

Supported example families should include at minimum:

- job seeker;
- office/admin/operations;
- customer service;
- healthcare support (with explicit privacy and human-review boundaries);
- trades/field service;
- marketing/content;
- small-business owner/operator;
- audio/creative work.

Role adaptation must not fabricate professional requirements or imply that AI replaces licensed, regulated, safety-critical, hiring, financial or clinical judgment.

## Feedback standard

Feedback must identify:

- what is already usable;
- what is missing or unclear;
- what requires factual verification;
- what creates a privacy/safety risk;
- one concrete improvement;
- whether the artifact meets the stated completion rubric.

AI-generated feedback is assistance, not proof of factual correctness. Where a human review is promised, the product must not label an automated review as human.

## Proof and portability

Completion should produce a portable proof pack rather than only a badge. It should identify the learner's own work, the problem addressed, AI's role, human review, corrections made, and the final artifact. The badge represents AI Kollege course completion only.

## Ethical upgrade rules

An upgrade recommendation must be tied to a demonstrated gap or stated goal. It must explain:

- why the next product is relevant;
- what additional work/output it provides;
- what the current product already gives the learner;
- a free next action the learner can take first when appropriate.

Never use fake scarcity, fabricated outcomes, guaranteed earnings, guaranteed employment, or a button click as proof of conversion/value.

## Analytics and improvement

Measure useful learning behavior without treating page views as learning outcomes. Where analytics is enabled, distinguish starts from completed artifacts and verified business outcomes. Product changes should be informed by completion friction, abandoned steps, support questions, artifact quality and actual upgrade behavior—not engagement alone.
